## 1. Introduction
This report presents a comprehensive analysis of graduate outcomes for the Faculty of Business, Economics and Accountancy (FPEP) for the year 2025. The dashboard was developed using Microsoft Excel, utilizing data extracted from the university’s official graduate database.
The purpose of this analysis is to:
-	Evaluate graduate employability and marketability 
-	Identify strengths and gaps across academic programs 
-	Support strategic decision-making for improving teaching and learning systems 
-	Enhance the overall quality of graduates produced by the faculty 

## 2. Data Preparation and Cleaning
Prior to analysis, a thorough data cleaning process was conducted to ensure accuracy and reliability:
-	Removed duplicate records 
-	Handled missing or incomplete values 
-	Standardized categorical variables (e.g., employment status, program names) 
-	Verified consistency between total graduates and categorized data 
It is important to note that PhD (Doctor of Philosophy) graduates are excluded from CGPA-based analysis, as they are not evaluated using pointer (CGPA). Therefore, totals for CGPA-related charts may differ from overall graduate counts.

## 3. Overview of Graduate Statistics
The dashboard highlights key graduate indicators through summary cards:
-	Total Graduates: 258 
-	Premium Income Graduates (≥ RM4,000): 61 
-	Graduates Earning <RM4,000: 156 
-	Graduates with No Income: 41 
Insights
-	Approximately 23.6% of graduates achieved premium income levels 
-	A significant proportion (60%) are earning below RM4,000 
-	41 graduates without income indicate a need for targeted employability interventions 

## 4. Employment Status Distribution
A pie chart illustrates the employment status of graduates:
-	Employed: 85% 
-	Unemployed: 7% 
-	Further Studies: 4% 
-	Skills Enhancement: 2% 
-	Awaiting Placement: 2%

Insights
-	The faculty demonstrates a strong employability rate (85%) 
-	However, the 7% unemployment rate should be addressed through targeted strategies 
-	A small percentage pursuing further studies suggests potential to promote postgraduate pathways 

## 5. Job Relevance to Field of Study
A bar chart presents alignment between employment and field of study:
-	Working in Relevant Field: 179 graduates 
-	Working in Non-Relevant Field: 40 graduates 
-	Not Applicable: 39 graduates

Insights
Majority of graduates are employed in relevant fields, indicating program effectiveness 

However, 40 graduates working outside their field may reflect: 
-	Skills mismatch 
-	Industry demand shifts 
-	Need for curriculum enhancement 

## 6. CGPA vs Employment Outcome Analysis
6.1 Graduates with CGPA < 3.5
-	Employed: 131 
-	Unemployed: 11 
-	Further Studies: 4 
-	Skills Enhancement: 5 
-	Awaiting Placement: 6

6.2 Graduates with CGPA ≥ 3.5
-	Employed: 48 
-	Unemployed: 5 
-	Further Studies: 2

Insights
-	Employment is not solely dependent on academic performance 
-	Graduates with lower CGPA still demonstrate strong employability 
-	High CGPA graduates show slightly better employment stability 

## 7. Program-Level Unemployment Analysis
A horizontal bar chart identifies unemployment by program.
Key finding:
-	Bachelor of Business (Hons) in Tourism Management recorded 4 unemployed graduates, the highest among programs 

This program may require: 
-	Curriculum review 
-	Industry collaboration strengthening 
-	Internship and job placement enhancement 


## 8. Interactive Dashboard Features
The dashboard includes:
-	Program slicer (left panel) to filter and analyze performance by specific programs 
-	Dynamic visualizations that update based on user selection 
-	User-friendly and minimal design tailored for management-level insights 

## 9. Key Findings
-	Strong overall employability rate (85%) 
-	Income distribution indicates room for improving graduate earning potential 
-	Majority of graduates work in relevant fields 
-	Certain programs show higher unemployment and require attention 
-	CGPA is not the sole determinant of employability 

## 10. Recommendations
To enhance graduate quality and employability:

Academic Improvement
-	Revise curriculum to align with industry demand 
-	Integrate more practical and industry-based learning

Industry Collaboration
-	Strengthen partnerships with employers 
-	Expand internship and job placement opportunities 

Career Development
-	Provide structured career support programs  
-	Enhance soft skills, communication, and digital competencies 
  
Program-Specific Intervention
-	Conduct detailed review of underperforming programs 
-	Implement targeted improvement strategies 

## 11. Conclusion
This analysis provides valuable insights into the performance of graduates from the Faculty of Business, Economics and Accountancy at Universiti Malaysia Sabah.

While employability levels are strong, there are clear opportunities to:
-	Improve income outcomes 
-	Reduce unemployment rates 
-	Strengthen program relevance 

By implementing targeted strategies, the faculty can further enhance the quality and marketability of its graduates in line with industry needs.
